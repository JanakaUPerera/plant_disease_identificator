# Experiment log

## P1 – Prediction (24 Oct 2026, before any model was trained)

**Expected winner on the primary metric (disease macro-F1, val_wild and test_wild): the ANN
(ImageNet-pretrained ResNet, fine-tuned).**

## Reasoning

1. The classical baseline uses manually designed colour and texture features extracted from the entire image. In laboratory images, the background is mostly plain, with only 1.1% of the border containing plant-coloured pixels. Therefore, these features mainly represent the leaf itself. However, in real-world images, 45% to 50% of the border contains other foliage. This introduces background information into the extracted features, which may reduce the model's ability to perform well on real-world images.

2. The CNN uses features pretrained on ImageNet, which contains natural images with different backgrounds and visual conditions. It can also identify local patterns, such as disease lesions and pustules, wherever they appear in the image. Therefore, it is expected to be less affected by differences in backgrounds and image framing.

3. Both models are expected to achieve high scores on `test_lab` because laboratory images have more controlled conditions and are easier to classify. However, the main performance difference is expected to appear when the models are evaluated on real-world images.

**Risk to This Prediction.**  
Although the CNN is pretrained on ImageNet, it is fine-tuned only on laboratory images. As a result, it may also learn features specific to laboratory conditions, such as plain backgrounds and controlled lighting. This could reduce its expected advantage when working with real-world images. If both models achieve scores close to the random guessing benchmark of 0.285, it would indicate that neither approach has learned features that transfer effectively to real-world conditions.

**Expected Performance Range.**  
The following values are initial estimates for disease macro-F1 on `val_wild`. They are predictions rather than measured results and are included to make the experimental expectations clear.

- ANN: 0.40–0.60
- Classical baseline: 0.30–0.40

### P2 – prediction for the final test (30 Oct 2026, before any test result)

**Expected Results Based on Validation Performance (`val_lab` and `val_wild` Only)**

1. The final ANN model is expected to achieve a higher F1-16 score on `test_wild` than both CLIP models. However, the expected difference is relatively small, at less than 0.10. The validation results show a similar difference of approximately 0.04–0.06.

2. The expected performance ranking on `test_wild`, based on F1-16, is: final ANN, followed by the CLIP models, the C04 classical model, and finally the random-within-crop benchmark.

3. Models trained using laboratory images (C04, R01, and the final ANN) are expected to achieve considerably lower scores on `test_wild` than on `test_lab`. In comparison, the CLIP models are expected to show a smaller performance difference between the two test sets, remaining within ±0.05.

4. The final ANN model is expected to achieve a lower F1-16 score on `test_wild` than its `val_wild` score of 0.471. This is expected because `val_wild` was used to select the best training epoch, while PlantWild contains images from a different source than PlantDoc.

5. OpenCLIP is expected to achieve higher late-blight recall than BioCLIP on `test_wild`. The validation results support this expectation, with recall values of 0.703 for OpenCLIP and 0.479 for BioCLIP.

6. After applying temperature scaling, R15 is expected to have a higher Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on `test_wild` than on `val_wild`, where the ECE is 0.047. However, the test ECE is still expected to remain below 0.10.
